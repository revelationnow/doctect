# Finding the right planner

*Episode 16 · In depth · 03:56*

## Welcome

The gallery has filled up since episode eleven. Today I'm shopping for a 2026 planner for my reMarkable Paper Pro. We'll narrow the gallery down, judge what's left, and take one home.

## Narrowing it down

These controls sit above every gallery view. Category comes first. Nobody files anything here: the gallery works out each project's category from its tags and name. Planners.

Size or device matters most for a tablet. Some entries cover several devices that share a screen, like the 10.3-inch one, because one file fits them all. Mine's the Paper Pro.

Results start sorted by Trending: what people are opening and forking lately. Newest and Most downloaded are here too. Top rated weighs each average by how many reviews back it, so one lucky five-star review can't outrank lots of good ones.

4+ keeps projects averaging four stars or more. Notice what else drops out: anything nobody has rated yet.

List view puts the same results in a table, so sizes and ratings sit side by side.

Everything I've picked is written into the page's address. Bookmark it, or send it to a friend, and it opens this exact list, signed in or not.

Back in the grid, tags are filters too. But a tag click starts a fresh view: planner is now the only filter, and the A4 and unrated planners are back. So pick a tag first, then narrow.

I want a 2026 planner, so I'll search for 2026. While there's a search, Sort switches to Best match, which puts names that match first.

## Is it any good?

Its page answers the first question: will it fit? It lists every size it comes in, the Paper Pro among them, and its page count: 1,368.

Then: is it any good? It averages 4.7 stars, from three reviews, and anyone can read them. Mei gave four, and wants dot grids on the notes pages. Stars alone can't tell you that.

The author's name links to a profile. Priya's shows a username, a join date, and what Priya has published. That's all: no real name, no email.

## Leaving a review

Back to Priya's planner, to add a review of my own. Writing one takes an account: Sign in to review goes to the sign-in page, and brings me straight back.

Reviews are signed with your username. The stars are the only required part; the words are optional. Five stars, and one line about why.

Save review, and the average moves straight away: 4.8, from four reviews. The heading now says Your review: there's one per person, and saving again replaces it.

One more rule: you can't review your own work. On your own projects this form never appears, and the server turns the review away anyway.

## Taking it home

Now, taking it home. Download all variants renders every edition right here in the browser, and hands over one zip. For this planner, that's three editions of 1,368 pages each, so it takes a few seconds.

Inside, there's one PDF per variant, named after it. The Paper Pro one is mine.

Here's that one in a PDF reader: the same linked planner, at my tablet's size.

Version history lists the versions Priya published, newest first, each with its note. Saves that were never published don't appear.

Open in editor on the older one puts that exact version in my editor, as a new tab. Like any Open in editor copy, it's mine, with no link back.

That's a planner found, checked and taken home. Next time: what happens when two people change the same thing.
