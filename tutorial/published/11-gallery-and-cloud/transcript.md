# Gallery, accounts & cloud history

*Episode 11 · Share & collaborate · 02:45*

## Welcome

So far, everything we have made has lived in this one browser. This episode steps outside it: the gallery, where people share what they make, and an account, which gives your work a history in the cloud.

## The gallery

Gallery, at the top, is open to everyone. No account, no sign in. It opens on a spotlight, then rows: what's trending, what's new, and a row for each kind of project.

Search reads names, tags and descriptions. I'm after something for dinners, so: meal.

Every project has a page: the preview pages its author picked, who made it, and its tags. Download all variants gets every PDF in one zip, and Version history opens any version the author published.

Open in editor copies the project into your own editor, as a new tab. The copy is yours: change anything you like, and the original never knows.

## An account

Our recipe book, though, is still only in this browser. On its tab, the Cloud menu has just one thing to offer while we are signed out: Sign in to save to cloud.

Sign Up turns the form into Create Account. Your name and your email stay private. The username is public: it is the name the gallery shows on everything you share.

The password needs at least twelve characters. Then Sign Up, and instead of signing you in, it sends a link to your email.

Here it is. Clicking the link confirms the address and signs you in, all in one go.

## Saving to the cloud

We're back in the editor, signed in, and the Cloud menu now offers Save to cloud. Nothing is ever uploaded behind your back. Each save is a snapshot you choose to make, with a message that says what it is.

Let's change something we can see. Tomato Soup becomes Roasted Tomato Soup, and we save again, with a message that says so. The message is for future you: in a few months, Roast the tomatoes will still mean something, and Update won't.

Version history lists every save, newest first. The top one is marked HEAD: the latest version in the cloud.

Restore puts an older version back in the editor. It asks first, because anything you had not saved would be lost. And there is plain Tomato Soup again.

Restoring only changed this tab. The cloud still has both versions, and saving now would simply add a third, so you can step back and forth as often as you like.

## My projects

Everything the cloud holds for you is on one page: My projects, in the account menu. Each project is private until you publish it, and the bar on top is your storage: fifty megabytes for every account.
