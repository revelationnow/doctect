# Your first planner, start to finish

*Episode 1 · Foundations · 03:48*

## Welcome

Hi. Let's make a planner. Not a mock-up, a real one: more than thirteen hundred pages, where every date links to its own page. We'll start from a preset, look around the editor, and export it. You won't need an account for any of this.

This is PDF Architect. The idea is simple. You design a handful of page layouts once, and the app turns them into hundreds of pages, with the links worked out for you.

Let's open the editor.

## Creating a project

The first time you open it, you get a blank project to play with. I want something with content in it, so I'll click the plus, next to the tab.

There are three presets. Blank is a single, empty A4 page. Simple Notebook has a cover and a few section dividers. And the 2026 Planner is the big one: a year, broken into quarters, months and days, all linked together. That's the one we want.

It takes a few seconds to build. And there it is: a new tab, with every page already in place.

## Finding your way around

The editor has three columns. On the left is the structure of the document: a tree of pages, which the app calls nodes. In the middle, the canvas shows one page at a time. And on the right are the properties of whatever you've selected.

Right now the top node is selected, so the canvas shows the year page. The properties panel says this node uses a template called Year View, and it carries one piece of data: the year.

## Drilling down

Under the year sit four quarters, then pages for weeks, notes, and to-do lists. The little arrow unfolds a node. Inside Quarter 1 are January, February and March.

Click January, and the canvas switches to a month: a calendar where every day is a link, and the week numbers down the side are links too.

Unfold January and there's a page for every day. Here's January the first. And each day has two pages of its own underneath it: a journal, and daily notes.

## Templates do the work

Now, here's the part that matters. Nobody sat down and drew 365 day pages. Switch the sidebar to Templates, and you can see the entire planner comes from just ten layouts.

I'll pick Month View. Up in the toolbar there's now a Preview menu. It lists every node that uses this template, and if I choose June, the same layout fills in with June.

That's really the whole model. Nodes hold the structure and the data. Templates hold the design. Change a template once, and every page that uses it changes with it.

## Exporting

Let's export it. The Export PDF button, up in the corner, runs every node through its template and gives you a single file.

Even at this size it only takes a few seconds, and it all happens right here in the browser, so nothing is uploaded anywhere. The buttons next to it handle greyscale and other devices. We'll get to those later in the series.

## Trying the links

Here's the file, open in a PDF reader. Page one is the year. These boxes aren't just labels. Click Quarter 2, and you land on its page.

Each of these little calendars is clickable too. Here is the second of May. From a day, the arrows step forward or back, and the chips along the top jump up to the month, the quarter, or the year.

None of these links were drawn by hand. They're worked out from the structure, which is why they're right on every single page.
