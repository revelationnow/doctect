# Onto your tablet

*Episode 14 · In depth · 04:31*

## Welcome

You've got a planner, or a notebook you made, and an e-ink tablet. There are two ways onto it, for two different jobs. I'm starting from the 2026 Planner preset, so there's no design work.

## Two ways onto a tablet

The first is the linked PDF. Export PDF, from episode one, makes the whole planner one file: all 1,368 pages, every link working. You open it on the tablet and write on its pages.

Its pages come out at the size of the variant you're on. This planner has one, Default, sized for a reMarkable Paper Pro. A tablet of another shape needs its own variant: that's episode nine.

The second is note templates: page designs your tablet draws behind your handwriting, in any notebook. Each page becomes an image at the tablet's exact pixel size, with no links.

So: the PDF when you want the planner itself, dated and linked. Templates when you want its page designs in the tablet's own notebooks.

## Choosing pages

The picker lists pages first, grouped by template. Each group starts with its first page ticked, so you get one of each design.

That's the usual want: one page of each design, not 365 copies of the Day View. I don't need the year page, so none clears its group.

Search reaches the pages tucked under more. I'd like December's month page too. In the search box, Escape clears it and keeps the tick. Anywhere else, Escape closes the picker.

One catch: a template is a picture of a page, so a dated page keeps its date. Use December's, and every page you write on says December. So I'll keep the note and to-do pages, which suit any day, and none clears the rest. That includes the journal and daily notes pages: their top bar shows the month.

## Choosing devices

Below the pages, Rescale to lists one option per screen, grouped by brand. Until one is ticked, Export waits. I'll tick Paper Pure. The reMarkable 2 under it has the same screen, so it gets the same file.

Color screens get color templates; every other model gets grey, converted like episode six's greyscale export. So the Paper Pure comes out grey, and the Boox Note Air C keeps its color.

Force greyscale makes them all grey. And ticking the Boox brought up a new box: Also include a PDF template.

It puts your pages in one PDF beside the images, with the links between them still working. reMarkable folders never get one. I'll add a Supernote Manta, and tick it.

## Count and limits

The line at the bottom keeps count: two pages on three devices, six templates. The ceiling is 400 images per export, about what a phone browser can zip.

Ticking all on the Day View asks for every day of the year: too many files, and Export waits. Then none clears the group, and we're back under.

## Export

Export draws every image right here in the browser, and the line counts them off. Cancel would stop it, with nothing downloaded. Your devices and both boxes are remembered for next time; the page ticks start fresh.

## What's in the zip

One zip, one folder per model, each laid out the way that device expects. That's png and methods for the reMarkable, MyStyle for the Manta, and noteTemplate for the Boox.

Here's the Paper Pure's to-do page: 1404 × 1872 pixels, the tablet's exact screen, in grey.

Each folder has an INSTALL.txt. Here's the Manta's: connect by USB, copy the MyStyle folder across, then pick a page under Templates, Customization.

That copying is yours to do: the app never touches your tablet. And reMarkable is the hard one, with no official way to add templates at all.

So its INSTALL.txt opens with a warning: both routes need SSH, at your own risk. Start with methods: line drawings that survive software updates; the PNGs look exact, but an update removes them. On a Paper Pro, getting SSH at all means a factory reset. The linked PDF needs none of that.

That's both ways onto your tablet. Next time: keeping your work safe, in the browser and out of it.
