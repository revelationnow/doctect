# Canvas essentials: designing a cover page

*Episode 2 · Foundations · 04:24*

## Welcome

Last time we explored a finished planner. This time we start with an empty page and make something of our own: the cover of a family recipe book. Along the way you'll meet every drawing tool, and the few rules the canvas plays by.

This is a fresh, blank project: one A4 page. First, a name. The tab takes its name from the top page, so I'll call it Family Recipes.

And I'll zoom out one step, so the whole page fits on screen.

## The tools

Across the top are the tools. Select, and the hand, for panning. Then text, rectangle, ellipse, triangle, line, and the data grid, which gets an episode of its own. Each one has a single-letter shortcut.

## Drag, don't click

Press R for the rectangle. Now, here's the rule that catches everyone the first time. A single click draws nothing. You have to drag.

Press, drag to the opposite corner, and let go.

The tool stays active after you draw, so dragging again makes a second rectangle, not a moved first one. Control Z takes it back.

## Selecting and moving

Press V for the select tool, and click the rectangle. You get eight handles for resizing, a rotate handle on top, and this small dot in the middle: the pivot it turns around.

Drag it to move it. The arrow keys nudge it one point at a time, or ten with Shift held.

For exact numbers, Geometry, on the right, takes typed values. I want this card 24 points in from every edge: X and Y of 24, a width of 547, and a height of 794.

## Fills and borders

Now the look. Fill can be a color, or a pattern. I'll switch it to Pattern, choose Dots, and pick a soft peach.

That's dot grid paper, made from one rectangle. The PDF stores it as a single repeating pattern rather than thousands of dots, so the file stays small.

For the border, a darker orange, two points wide, and dashed. And Radius rounds the corners off.

## Text and fonts

Press T for text. Text is the one tool where a single click does make something: a small box that grows as you type. For a title, though, I'd rather drag out the full width, so it stays centered.

Type the title, then press Escape. That commits the text and drops you back to the select tool.

The font picker is searchable. I'll type play, and take Playfair Display. After that, a bigger size, bold, and a deep brown.

Now a subtitle, dragged out the same way. Notice it starts with the same font, size and color as the title. The text tool remembers your last settings. This one needs to be smaller, so 18 points, and italic instead of bold.

## Shapes and alignment

A circle for a little decoration. E for ellipse, and hold Shift as you drag, to keep it perfectly round.

A solid peach fill, and no border.

And a short line under the title. L for line, and drag it across.

Is everything centered? Not quite. I'll select it all by dragging a box from outside the page, and the toolbar grows a row of alignment buttons. Align Center lines up every middle, halfway between the outermost edges, which here are the card's.

## Zoom, pan and snap

To look closer, hold Control and scroll. The zoom centers on wherever the pointer is. The hand tool, H, drags the view around, and so does holding down the middle mouse button.

The magnet turns on snapping, and shows the grid it snaps to. The grid gets finer as you zoom in, so it doesn't fight you on detail work. I'll switch both off again.

## Undo, and saving

If anything goes wrong, Control Z steps back, and Control Y steps forward again. And there is no save button to forget. This badge shows the project is saved in your browser as you work.
