# Publishing, forks & merge requests

*Episode 12 · Share & collaborate · 03:37*

## Welcome

Our recipe book has a history in the cloud now, but only we can see it. This episode is about sharing: publishing to the gallery, forking someone else's work, and taking in changes a friend proposes to ours.

## Publishing

Publish to gallery, in the Cloud menu, puts the latest cloud version on a public page. A description and a few tags are how people will find it: search reads both, and every tag becomes a link to everything else that shares it. There's no category to pick. The gallery works that out from the tags and the name.

Then the preview pages, up to six. The first one you tick becomes the cover on the gallery card, and the cover page is ticked already. I'll add Tomato Soup.

Publish draws the previews, uploads them, and the book is live. Each preview is a real render of the page, made in your browser, so a big project takes a moment.

Here it is in the gallery, under New. Its page shows exactly what we chose. And because it's ours, there's an Edit listing button, which changes the description, tags and previews without publishing anything new.

One thing to remember: the gallery shows the version you published. Anything you save after that stays private, until you publish again.

## Forking

Now someone else's work. Sam's meal planner could do with brighter day titles. Signed in, its page offers one more button: Fork this project.

A fork is a copy in your own cloud that remembers where it came from. It opens in the editor like any other project.

The Cloud menu knows the difference. It says this is forked from upstream, with a link back to Sam's page, and it offers Propose changes to upstream.

First, the change itself. In Templates, I'll open the Day template and select its title. Orange, from the color picker. Then it has to be saved to the cloud, because a merge request always proposes your latest save, never what's only in this tab.

Propose changes to upstream asks for a title and a short note about what changed, and why. Sam will read it before he sees a single page, so write it the way you'd want to receive it.

That makes a merge request, with a page of its own. It lists the changes, here one template, and it waits for Sam: he can merge it, or close it. It's his project, so it's his call.

## Reviewing a merge request

Meanwhile, Sam forked our book too. He added a recipe, and proposed it back to us. Merge requests for your projects are listed on their gallery pages, where only you can see them.

Its page lists what changed: the page hierarchy, because Sam added a page. Render before and after draws a page both ways, so you can see the change, not just read about it.

Merge makes a new version of our project with Sam's recipe in it. It asks first.

## Bringing it home

The merge happened in the cloud. The book open in our editor hasn't changed, and that's on purpose: nothing changes your work behind your back. Try to save, and the cloud says it has moved on, and overwrites nothing.

Reload cloud version brings the merged book into the editor. And there's Sam's Shakshuka: on the cover's index, and as a page of its own.

Publish again whenever you want the gallery to have it too. And that is the series: from a first planner, to a book that people share and improve together. Thanks for watching.
